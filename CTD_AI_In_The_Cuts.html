How runtime was measured: the base doesn't store a master-duration field, but every AI clip carries its timeline Seq-Out timecode on a broadcast master that starts at 01:00:00:00. The last AI clip in each cut therefore marks elapsed program time. Because a program can run a little past its final AI shot, these runtimes are a floor — so the true AI share is these figures or slightly lower.

101 needed 5.61 generations per clip that made the cut. 102 dropped to 2.47, 103 to 2.53, 104 to 1.75 — and the episodes with the most AI on screen are now the leanest to produce. Same or better output for a third of the generation waste.

Read with two caveats: (1) 101 has been in production longest, so it has accumulated the most experimental and re-spun generations — some of its 1,896 is early-days learning that later episodes skip. (2) "Used" counts timeline placements; a clip used twice counts twice, so true distinct-clip efficiency is marginally leaner still.
