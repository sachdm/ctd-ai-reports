Yes — sharply. The generate-to-use ratio has more than halved across three episodes. Same on-screen output for less than half the generation waste. That's the locked prompt library, the reference-frame workflow, and re-spin discipline compounding.
Two caveats for honesty: (1) 101 has been in production longest and carries the most one-time experimental and re-spun generations — some of its 5.1:1 was tuition later episodes skip. (2) "In cut" counts timeline placements; a clip used twice counts twice, so true distinct-clip efficiency is marginally leaner. The trend is unambiguous either way.

5   Method & sources

  • AI share: sum of AI clip durations per cut (GEN AI REPORTS), excluding rows flagged outdated or duplicate, over the runtime taken from each cut's final Seq-Out timecode.
  • Efficiency: total delivered files per episode's stories (GEN AI ASSETS, matched by story-code) divided by AI placements in that episode's cut (GEN AI REPORTS).
  • Pipeline: live status counts from GEN AI SHOTCODES; re-spin count from GEN AI ASSETS re-spin statuses.
  • Scope: 1,523 clip placements, 3,990 generated files, 1,380 planned shots read. Two figures are floors (runtime, placements-as-clips) and are flagged where they appear; both bias the same direction and don't change any conclusion.
